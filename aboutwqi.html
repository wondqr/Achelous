Water Quality Index

A report on water quality presented in a format that's easy to understand, so anyone can see how clean the water is at Assumption College and whether it could affect health. The index combines four key factors - temperature, pH, TDS, and turbidity - into one simple reading.

The Four Parameters

Temperature

How hot or cold the water is - a basic physical property that affects everything else in the water.

  • Cold Water: 10 - 15 °C
  • Room Temperature: 20 - 25 °C
  • Warm Water: 40 - 50 °C

pH Value

A scale that shows how acidic or alkaline the water is.

  • pH < 7 → Acid
  • pH = 7 → Neutral
  • pH > 7 → Base

Total Dissolved Solids (TDS)

The total amount of dissolved organic and inorganic substances present in the water.

Turbidity

How cloudy the water looks, caused by suspended solids or sediment particles.

Standard Reference Values

Quality Parameter Average Clean Water Range Why It Matters
Temperature 5 - 40 °C Affects dissolved oxygen, chemical reaction rates, microbial activity, and indirectly pH and conductivity.
pH Value 6.5 - 8.5 Affects heavy metal solubility and acts as an early warning sign of contamination.
TDS Value 0 - 150 ppm Doesn't identify specific contaminants — high TDS may come from harmless minerals or harmful substances — but can affect taste.
Turbidity < 1 NTU Indicates possible microbial contamination and warns of issues before detailed testing.

Calculation of the daily air quality index

The WQI turns the sensor readings into a single score from 0 to 100. Each parameter is first converted into its own sub-index q, then the sub-indices are combined using weights.

1Combine the sub-indices

The final WQI is the weighted sum of the sub-indices of pH, TDS, and turbidity.

\[ WQI = \sum \left( w_i \times q_i \right) = w_{pH}\,q_{pH} + w_{TDS}\,q_{TDS} + w_{turbidity}\,q_{turbidity} \]

pH0.34
TDS0.33
Turbidity0.33
Temperature is measured and displayed on the monitor, but it is not included in the WQI score.

2Find the band of each reading

Compare each measured value with the ranges below to find which water-quality band it falls in. The limits of that band are then used in the sub-index formula.

  • Vactual = the value measured by the sensor
  • Vmin = the lowest sensor value of that band
  • Vmax = the highest sensor value of that band
  • Qmin = the lowest WQI score of that band
  • Qmax = the highest WQI score of that band

Example: TDS = 120 ppm falls in the Clean band, so Vmin = 101 ppm, Vmax = 150 ppm, Qmin = 70, and Qmax = 84.

3Calculate each sub-index (q)

Use the limits of the band to place the reading between Qmin and Qmax. The formula depends on the parameter.

TDS, Turbidity, and pH ≥ 7 (a higher reading gives a lower score)

\[ q_i = \frac{V_{min} - V_{actual}}{V_{max} - V_{min}} \times \left( Q_{max} - Q_{min} \right) + Q_{max} \]

pH < 7 (a reading closer to 7 gives a higher score)

\[ q_i = \frac{V_{actual} - V_{min}}{V_{max} - V_{min}} \times \left( Q_{max} - Q_{min} \right) + Q_{min} \]
Special cases:
  • A value that falls in a small gap between two rows (for example TDS 100.5 ppm) uses the next band, and q is capped at that band's Qmax.
  • A value beyond the highest band (TDS > 750 ppm, turbidity > 100 NTU, or pH outside 0 - 14) gets q = 0.
